The gap

Both sides want to engage. Few know how to start.

Most collaborations depend on personal contacts and one-off MoUs. Many are signed, and fewer are carried through and documented.

Institutions

  • Hard to judge which startups are credible and relevant
  • CoEs launched with events but not sustained
  • Industry engagement is poorly documented for accreditation
  • Unclear norms on IP, fees and timelines

Startups

  • No clear route to the right department or decision-maker
  • Slow processes and unclear terms for pilots
  • Need skilled interns, not mass applications
  • Edtech teams need a channel that is not a cold call
Solutions

Six ways to work together, on one platform

Existing portals usually cover a single type of collaboration. Here every engagement starts from the same verified profile and the same agreement templates.

Centres of Excellence

Blueprint with objectives, equipment, curriculum, staffing and a three-year plan, plus partners to build and run it.

Course and lecture delivery

Self-study, partial-delivery and skill-enhancement courses mapped to your syllabus and credit scheme.

Internships and live projects

Startups state the skills they need; departments shortlist students and track credits and feedback.

Sponsored projects

Startups bring a problem, faculty bring the method, and the agreement settles IP and fees up front.

Faculty consulting

Short advisory roles for faculty with clear conflict-of-interest and IP rules.

Lab and facility access

Startups use campus labs, and institutions see who is using what and with what result.

Pilot roadmap

A staged plan, built on evidence

We begin with a small, curated group and expand only when the results justify it.

Phase 1

Validate

Interviews with principals, HoDs, founders and placement officers to confirm the top needs.

Phase 2

Curated pilot

Bengaluru institutions and startups in two or three domains, matched by hand.

Phase 3

Build and expand

Templates, matching and reporting built into the platform, then more colleges across Karnataka.

Leadership

Led from within the academic community

An advisory panel is being formed of principals, a legal and IP advisor, a startup founder and an incubator head.

FAQ

Common questions

Is the pilot free?

Yes. Founding pilot partners join at no cost while we validate the model. Any paid tiers will be announced in advance.

Who can join?

Engineering colleges, universities and departments, and startups and edtech companies, with a focus on Bengaluru and Karnataka.

How are partners verified?

We check institutional or startup registration details and speak to a named contact before listing a profile.

Who owns the IP in a project?

That is settled in the agreement before work begins. The platform provides standard templates, and parties may adapt them with legal advice.

Are you affiliated with a company?

This is an independent academic-led pilot. Any affiliations of the organiser are disclosed, and recommendations follow transparent criteria.

Tell us what you need. We will reply personally and shortlist the first matches for your domain.
